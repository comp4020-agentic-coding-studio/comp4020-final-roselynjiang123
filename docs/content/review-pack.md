## Learning objectives

- **LO1** Check the total amount prepared using units, strength and quantity, and tell a different way of writing the same dose from a different dose.
- **LO2** Use the order, the administration record and relevant history such as allergies to explain why matching records can still mean pausing to clarify.
- **LO3** Choose a next step with evidence, and in the joint debrief tell apart agreeing, matching the reference answer, and real-world safety.

## The five checks, next steps and evidence options

- **Patient** — Do the patient identifiers on the prepared medicine match the chart and the ID band?
- **Medicine** — Is the prepared medicine the one that was ordered?
- **Dose** — Does the total amount prepared equal the ordered dose?
- **Route & form** — Does the form prepared fit the ordered route?
- **Time on record** — Does the time or schedule on the prepared record match the order?

_These five checks compare the order with what was prepared. Whether a dose should be given now (for example, how long since the last dose, or an allergy) is part of your next step and evidence, not these five checks._

Next steps: **Continue with the remaining checks** (Nothing here stops this dose. Carry on with this scenario's remaining bedside checks. This is not permission to give a real medicine.); **Stop and re-prepare** (The order is fine, but what was prepared isn't. Prepare it again and re-check.); **Pause and clarify** (Don't give this dose now. Sort it out with your facilitator, the nurse in charge or the prescriber.)

Evidence options (the same list in every case): The order and the prepared medicine match on all five checks; The total amount prepared (strength × quantity); The patient identifiers (name, date of birth, MRN); The administration record (when the last dose was given); The allergy record; The route or form prepared; What the patient said

## Before-you-start card

- **Units**: 1 g = 1000 mg. 1 mg = 1000 micrograms. Write “micrograms” and “units” in full: “µg” and “U” are easily misread. Convert to the same unit before you compare.
- **Total amount**: Tablets and capsules: strength × number. Liquids: strength (mg per mL) × volume (mL). Example with other numbers: 2 mg/mL × 3 mL = 6 mg.
- **Routes and forms**: Oral: swallowed. Inhaled: breathed in from an inhaler, often through a spacer. Nebulised: a liquid turned into mist through a mask. IV: into a vein. Subcutaneous: under the skin. A different form can mean a different route and different units.
- **PRN orders**: PRN means “when required”. A PRN order says the shortest gap between doses and may give a maximum for 24 hours. In this practice, use the interval written on each order; don't assume one rule for every medicine.
- **Reading the chart**: The allergy box is at the top of the chart. The order says what, how, how much and when. The administration record shows what was actually given, with the date and time. Count across midnight using the date as well as the time.
- **Patient identifiers**: Use three: full name, date of birth and MRN. Ask the patient to tell you their name and date of birth, rather than asking “Are you…?”.
- **This practice**: First compare the order with what was prepared, on five checks. Then choose a next step and the evidence that decided it. Your partner can't see your answers until you've both signed.
