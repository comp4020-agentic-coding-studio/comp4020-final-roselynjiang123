# Facilitator guide

Facts only, drawn from the build brief and the current case bank. The
student should edit this before using it with a class.

## Goals

Second Pair of Eyes is a two-person, real-time teaching game for nursing
students: paired medication-check practice on a simulated adult ward. Two
students each check the same six fictional medication tasks on their own,
sign, then see together what each of them found, what both missed, and why.
A short training summary, three new transfer situations and an end review
follow.

The exercise is built to:

1. Protect independent judgement — before both sign, nobody can see the
   partner's answer, the reference answer or any feedback.
2. Make "who found what" visible — agreement, accuracy, complementary finds
   and shared misses are kept as separate, not combined into one score.
3. Turn findings into a next step with evidence, keeping "the records
   match" apart from "this dose should be given now".
4. Be honest about content and effect — every case shows its version,
   sources and real review status; nothing claims clinical effect.

## Who it is for

Nursing students practising medication-record comparison, evidence-based
discussion and clarification, working in pairs. It is a simulation, not a
clinical tool, not a competence assessment, and not evidence that double
checking works.

## A 20–25 minute plan

A full session (six training cases, the training summary, three transfer
situations, the end review) takes about 20–25 minutes.

1. Before starting: both students open the site in their own browser
   profile (see "Two-browser set-up" below).
2. One student creates a table and sends the invite link to their partner.
3. Both read the prebrief and "Before you start" card.
4. Both independently check each of the six training cases, sign, then see
   the joint debrief together — repeated six times.
5. After case 6, both see the training summary.
6. Either student starts the three new transfer situations; same
   independence and reveal rules apply.
7. After the third transfer situation, both see the end review, which shows
   training and transfer results separately.

## Two-browser set-up

Each student needs their own browser identity — an anonymous,
server-issued session cookie, not a login. Two Chrome profiles (or two
different browsers) on the same or different machines both work. The
person creating the table copies the invite link and sends it to their
partner, who opens it and clicks Join.

## Keeping independence until both have signed

Nobody can see their partner's answer, the reference answer, or any
feedback until both of them have signed the current case. If the two
students are in the same room, they should sit apart or back to back so
neither can see the other's screen while checking.

## How to read the summary

The training summary (after case 6) and the end review (after the three
transfer situations) report:

- Mismatches found, as a fraction (how many of the real mismatches on the
  tray you found), never a percentage, and never averaged across cases.
- False marks — matching items you marked as a mismatch.
- Decisions accepted — how many cases your next step and evidence matched
  an accepted answer.
- Fully correct cases — cases where all five checks and your decision were
  right.
- Between-you figures — what you found together, what you both missed, and
  where your next-step choices agreed or differed.
- A zero denominator (no real opportunity for a given field) is reported as
  "No opportunity this time", never as 0% or 100%.

## The three new situations

After the training summary, the table moves to three new patient
situations (not part of the original six training cases). These are
counted and reported separately from training, so the summary can show
whether the checking method carries over to something the student hasn't
seen before, rather than mixing it into the training score.

## Not using results for competence decisions

A correct response means agreement with this exercise's reference answer
for this scenario, not proof that a real-world medication decision is
safe. Results here must not be used to make decisions about a student's
clinical competence. See the full scope statement on the Sources page.
