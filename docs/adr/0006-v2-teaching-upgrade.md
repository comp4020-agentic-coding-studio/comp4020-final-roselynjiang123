# 0006: v2 teaching upgrade — rule-based feedback, not runtime AI

**Context.** v1.2 shipped a single-round, five-field match/mismatch game
with a server-authored case bank (`src/cases.mjs`) and a fixed pair-review
sentence set. The student authorised a v2 upgrade (this round): real
medicine content externally versioned in `content/case-bank.v2.json`, a
nine-case training+transfer flow across four stages, a next-step-plus-
evidence decision on top of the five fields, and per-session summaries with
targeted suggestions. `docs/build-brief.md` v2 §2 explicitly rules out
"live or runtime AI of any kind" and §8 requires the scoring/debrief/
summary layer to be pure functions reproducing `docs/content/scoring-
fixtures.json` exactly.

**Options.** (1) Keep v1: no new content model, no decision step, no
summaries — misses the brief's authorised scope entirely. (2) v2 with
runtime AI: generate feedback, debrief wording or suggestions from a live
model call per submission — ruled out by brief §2 and §14 ("add anything
from §2"), and incompatible with §8's "reproduces exactly" fixture
contract, since a model call is not deterministic. (3) v2 with rule-based
feedback: round results, debrief composition and session summaries as
pure functions over the bank's authored templates/feedback text and the
fixture file's normative rules, with no network or model call anywhere in
the scoring path.

**Decision.** Chose (3). `content/case-bank.v2.json` is the single source
of clinical content (student-owned wording, answers, review status);
`docs/content/scoring-fixtures.json` (generated by `tools/content/
reference_scoring.py`, not hand-edited) is the normative, deterministic
target for round results, debrief blocks and session summaries. T06–T08 and
T17 (brief §12) enforce this directly: exact fixture reproduction and no
outbound network calls during scoring.

**Consequences.** Feedback quality is bounded by what the bank's authors
write per case, not adapted to a learner's specific wrong answer beyond the
bank's pre-written Finding/Evidence/Why-it-matters/Next-step blocks and
template-selected headlines (§8.2's first-matching-rule approach). Adding
or changing feedback means editing the bank and re-running the reference
script, not adjusting a prompt. This keeps the system auditable (every
debrief sentence traces to a specific bank field) and testable (deterministic
output against fixed input), at the cost of per-case authoring effort
staying entirely on the student, per CLAUDE.md's content-governance rule.

## Student's judgement

I chose rule-based feedback because the app's fourth claim is honesty about its content. Every sentence a learner reads after a reveal already exists in `content/case-bank.v2.json`, carries a review status, and can be checked before anyone sees it. Runtime AI would make the debrief more responsive and could answer questions the bank never anticipated, which S1's participants asked for. But its clinical text could not be reviewed before it was shown, and T17 could no longer show that scoring makes no outbound calls. The cost is that feedback is only as good as what I wrote, and a question the bank didn't anticipate waits for the facilitator after the session.
